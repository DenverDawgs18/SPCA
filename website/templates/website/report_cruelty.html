Our Role

How cruelty investigations work

The Medina County SPCA employs humane agents who are authorized under Ohio law to investigate complaints of animal abuse and neglect. Our officers work closely with local law enforcement and can confiscate animals in danger and pursue criminal charges when necessary.

When you make a report, a humane agent will assess the situation. Not every case is clear-cut, and our first goal is always to educate owners and resolve situations peacefully. When that isn't enough, we have the authority to act.

What to include in your report

  • The address or location of the animal
  • A description of what you observed and when
  • Photos or video, if you have them and can share safely
  • Any information about the owner (name, vehicle, etc.)

Reports can be made anonymously, though providing your contact information helps us follow up if we have questions.

For life-threatening emergencies, always call 911 first. After the immediate danger is addressed, please also contact us so we can follow up on the animal's welfare.

What counts as cruelty or neglect?

Common situations our officers investigate include:

  • Animals left outside without adequate shelter, food, or water
  • Visible injuries with no apparent veterinary care
  • Animals confined in unsanitary or dangerous conditions
  • Animals left in a hot vehicle
  • Physical abuse witnessed or suspected
  • Hoarding situations with multiple animals in poor condition
  • Chained or tethered animals in unsafe conditions

If you're unsure whether what you're seeing qualifies, call us anyway. It's always better to report and let a trained officer assess the situation.
